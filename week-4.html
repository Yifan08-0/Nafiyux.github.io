WEEK 4 / WORKSHOP REFLECTION

Week 4 Data Visualisation

This page is reserved for my weekly reflection.

1. Organising the Results

This week, we continued looking at how UK universities ask students to disclose their use of generative AI in written assignments. We put data from 10 universities into one table and compared them across six criteria. For example, we recorded whether students had to disclose their AI use, name the tool, explain what they used it for, or follow a set declaration format. Putting these details side by side made it easier to see where the requirements differed.

2. Presenting the Data

If I turned the table into a chart, I would start with one question: which universities explicitly require disclosure, and which require it only in certain situations? I would also give codes 0 and 9 separate labels. A 0 means the guidance explicitly says disclosure is not required. A 9 means the guidance does not specify, or we could not tell from what we found. If I grouped them together, readers could easily mistake a gap in our evidence for a university policy.

3. What a Chart Can Leave Out

A chart would make the results quicker to compare, but some details would still need an explanation. For example, two universities might both be coded as “conditionally required” even though their conditions differ. Our table also includes notes for Oxford and Cambridge to distinguish university-wide guidance from college-level rules. If I published a chart on my portfolio, I would keep those notes next to it so readers could see what the codes mean in context.
