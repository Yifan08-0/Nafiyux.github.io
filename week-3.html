WEEK 3 / WORKSHOP REFLECTION

Week 3 Data, Power, and Classification

This page is reserved for my weekly reflection.

Group Task

Our group explored the question: “How do UK universities require students to disclose generative AI use in written assignments?” We discussed the coding framework together and used shared categories to organise different universities’ requirements. I was responsible for finding and recording information for the University of Leeds and the University of Bristol, and creating charts to present the data.

Finding and Coding Information

The main difficulty was finding official information and interpreting the different ways universities expressed their requirements. Some statements were clear, while others needed more context, making it difficult to decide which code to use. We needed to discuss whether the statements matched our coding definitions rather than making decisions based on individual words.

Understanding Classification

This task helped me realise that turning policy documents into data involves more than filling in a spreadsheet. Coding requires us to interpret the text and decide which category it belongs to. Shared categories make comparison easier, but they can also simplify conditions and details in the original documents. In particular, not finding a clear requirement is different from finding an explicit statement that disclosure is not required.
