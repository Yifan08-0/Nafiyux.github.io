WEEK 6 / WORKSHOP REFLECTION

Week 6 Algorithmic Identity

This page is reserved for my weekly reflection.

1. What Platforms See

I started by looking at the types of data Douyin records or says it collects, including my watch and search history, likes, favourites, accounts I follow, and posts I have recommended. I used to think mainly about what I posted, but even when I don’t post anything, these everyday actions leave a record. For the second part, I chose Taobao to think about how a platform might infer my shopping preferences. The products it recommended gave me some clues, but I couldn’t tell how Taobao had categorised me or which searches or clicks had influenced its recommendations.

2. Classifying Douyin Posts

Using Sumpter’s method, I classified the 15 most recent posts from each of the 32 Douyin accounts I follow, giving me 480 posts in total. Lifestyle was the largest category, with 166 posts, followed by Outdoors, with 106. Each post could go into only one category. For example, a video about camping with friends could go under either Outdoors or Friends, and that choice would change the impression the table gives of the account. I also realised it had been a long time since I’d looked closely at what my friends had been posting. I usually watch whatever comes up in my feed and rarely go to their profiles to look back over the past six months. This exercise gave me a chance to slow down and see what they had been doing and what they chose to share. I felt a little more caught up with their lives afterwards.

3. Can Data Represent a Person?

The exercise helped me understand what Cheney-Lippold means by algorithmic identity. When I put all 15 posts from one account into Lifestyle, the table gave me a clear impression of that account. But that impression came from the posts I selected and the categories I used. If I looked at a different period or used different categories, I might get a different picture. It made me think about my own Douyin history too: the platform can record what I searched for and watched, but those records don’t necessarily explain why I watched it.
