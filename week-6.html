WEEK 7 / WORKSHOP REFLECTION

Week 7 Looking Back at Sumpter’s Method

This page is reserved for my weekly reflection.

1. Looking Back at the Results

This week, we revisited the results of the exercise we did last week using Sumpter’s method. Looking back at the 480 Douyin posts I classified, I saw that Lifestyle was the largest category. I also noticed that I had put all 15 of one account’s most recent posts under Lifestyle, and all 15 from another account under Jokes/Memes. I did not classify any posts as Politics/News or Activism. The results show what these accounts had been posting recently, but the absence of a topic doesn’t mean that the people behind the accounts don’t care about it.

2. How I Got These Results

The results also reflect how I selected and classified the posts. Since I chose accounts I already follow, the sample may reflect my own interests. I could only assign each post to one category, so the spreadsheet shows my final choice without showing which other categories might also have made sense. If I did the exercise again, I would add a short note for any post that was hard to classify. I would record another category it could fit into and why I made my final choice. That way, when I looked back at the table, I could still explain how I arrived at the numbers.

3. Posts and Identity

Looking closely at my friends’ posts last week helped me catch up on what they had been doing. But when I look at the finished table, all I see is how many posts fell into each category. Many of the details that helped me learn more about my friends are missing. This made me think of Cheney-Lippold’s idea of algorithmic identity: when platforms classify people based on certain behaviours, those categories can influence what they see next. So I think it’s worth asking two questions: how accurate is the classification, and what might it change for the person being classified?
